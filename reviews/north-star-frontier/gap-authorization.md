# Gap analysis — Clause 3: "as its principal is willing to authorize"

Date: 2026-09-08. Static reading of /home/user/nucleus and /home/user/gatehouse; every line reference below was re-read from source (not taken from the maps). No builds run, no files modified.

## 0. Frame

Clause 3 is about the *language* and *carriers* of authorization: can a principal say exactly what they mean (scope, time, budget, counterparties, conditions, escalation paths); can that authority be attenuated and delegated offline-verifiably through subagents and across hosts; are approvals bound to specific actions rather than "yes"; can authority be revoked or killed; can several principals co-authorize; and can a third party verify after the fact who authorized what.

What nucleus has that is genuinely strong for this clause, and which every gap below builds on rather than replaces:

- `portcullis::certificate::LatticeCertificate` (`crates/portcullis/src/certificate.rs:115-180, 1344-1440`): Biscuit-shaped Ed25519 chain, ephemeral per-hop keys, hash-linked, per-block expiry, monotone `leq` re-verified per hop, `SinkScope` containment, proof-of-possession, sealed `VerifiedPermissions`, depth bound. Offline-verifiable from one root public key. Kani (3 harnesses, crypto stubbed) and Lean (`CertChainMonotoneExtracted`) on the monotone walk.
- Node `PodAuthority` (`crates/nucleus-node/src/pod_authority.rs:20-56`): the only minting point; re-roots external chains with RFC 8693 `act`-style provenance; leaf must equal the authenticated SPIFFE id; `BudgetLedger` conserves `Σ live child allocations + consumed ≤ max`.
- `ApprovalBundle` (`crates/nucleus-identity/src/approval_bundle.rs:111-160`): ES256 JWS with `jti/iss/iat/exp/manifest_hash/approved_operations/max_uses/drand_round/attestation_hash`, verified against pinned keys, fail-closed when none.
- `SignedTaskRef` (`crates/nucleus-provenance-memory/src/taskref_token.rs:70-88`) with `TokenScope{allowed_operations, allowed_paths}`; k-of-n `SignedDeclassify` (`declassify.rs:8-36`) with a zero-threshold refusal.
- The house discipline: ratchets, honest code comments (#2406 is documented *in* the refusal path), "roster, not a quorum" said out loud.

The gaps are where the language stops, where the carrier cannot express what a principal means, where approvals detach from actions, and where nothing can be undone.

## 1. Ranked gaps

### G1 — Approvals are op-named counters, not action-bound signed mandates; the runtime approval path is dead, unattributed, and self-serviceable  [critical / large / architectural]

**What is.**
- The runtime grant is `ApproveRequest{operation: String, count, expires_at_unix, nonce}` (`crates/nucleus-tool-proxy/src/main.rs:921-929`) stored in `ApprovalRegistry{approvals: Mutex<HashMap<String, ApprovalEntry>>}` (`main.rs:629-631`). In-process it is `BTreeMap<Operation, u32>` (`crates/portcullis/src/kernel.rs:469-470`), granted by `Kernel::grant_approval(op, count)` (`kernel.rs:1747-1750`) and spent by `consume_approval` (`kernel.rs:2025-2033`). The unit of approval is therefore an operation name (13-variant enum) plus a count, never the arguments (`PrimitiveAction::{EditFile{path,patch}, RunCommand{command}, WriteFile{path}, …}`, `crates/portcullis/src/action_term.rs:75-104`) that an `ActionTerm` already carries.
- The HTTP path is dead by the code's own admission: `Verdict::RequiresApproval` in `decide_with_flow_mapped` returns before `sandbox.write`, so "an operator who grants an approval here gets a 200 and no effect (#2406)" (`crates/nucleus-tool-proxy/src/mediation.rs:161-190`, corroborated at `main.rs:3105-3122`, which also notes the grant is consumed twice when the arm *is* reached).
- The record does not name the approver: `actor: ActorIdentity::Unknown` (`main.rs:4348`). The approver's Ed25519 signature is verified in the auth layer and then discarded.
- `/v1/escalate` validates, mints an `EscalationGrant`, logs a verdict and returns JSON; it never touches the kernel (`crates/nucleus-tool-proxy/src/escalate.rs:176-210`; no `kernel`/`attenuate` reference in the file). `EscalationGrant` (`crates/portcullis/src/escalation.rs:513-530`) has no signature field.
- Human UI: `/dev/tty` `Approve operation 'X'? [y/N]` on the operation name, then `kernel.grant_approval(op, 1)` (`crates/nucleus-mcp/src/main.rs:1072, 1382-1398`). The Python SDK `Session.approve(operation, action)` POSTs `/v1/approve` itself (`sdk/python/nucleus_sdk/session.py:138-160`) — the caller self-approves.
- Preflight bundles: the loader maps `max_uses: None` to `usize::MAX` grants (`main.rs:~845`, `claims.max_uses.map(|n| n as usize).unwrap_or(usize::MAX)`), keyed by op string; `verify()` does not check `jti` dedup, `drand_round` or `attestation_hash` (`approval_bundle.rs:355-470` only checks signature, `iat`, `exp`, `manifest_hash`).
- `Kernel::grant_approval` / `issue_approved_token` are unauthenticated `&mut self` methods (`kernel.rs:1747, 1905`).

**What SOTA does.** AP2 Cart Mandate: the principal signs the *exact* cart hash, auto-signable by the agent only inside a signed Intent Mandate's predicates (nucleus already frames this in `crates/nucleus-recompute/src/cart.rs:1-45` for carts, but not for effects). AuthZEN AARP and UCAN invocation: an approval is a signed object naming the invocation (args + nonce + chain). PORTICO: grants are opaque, epoch-bound handles removed on closure with stale-replay rejection before side effects.

**Direction.** Replace the counter with an *approval block*: a signed artifact `{approver_kid, chain_fingerprint, op, args_hash (from ActionTerm), sink_scope, nonce, exp, max_uses}` that is (a) verified by the same pinned-key logic as `ApprovalBundle`, (b) appended to the pod's delegation state as a monotone widening bounded by `EscalationPolicy.max_grant`, (c) consumed exactly once by `decide_term` on an `ActionTerm` whose `args_hash` matches, and (d) recorded as a signed lineage edge (see G6). Make `/v1/escalate` produce that block and have `Kernel` accept it via one authenticated method (kill `grant_approval(op, n)`). Route `http_kernel_decide`'s `RequiresApproval` through the registry (fix #2406) and drop the double consume. Return a structured `approval_required{op, args_hash, chain_fp}` so a UI (CIBA/elicitation adapter, not `/dev/tty`) can show the *action*.

### G2 — No revocation or kill-switch below "lock the fleet read-only"; time can be extended  [critical / medium / architectural]

**What is.** `verify_certificate` has six steps (depth, root sig, root expiry, per-block hash/sig/leq/scope/expiry, PoP, seal) and no revocation step (`certificate.rs:1344-1440`). `grep -i revok` over `certificate.rs`, `token.rs`, `kernel.rs`, all of `nucleus-tool-proxy/src`, and `pod_authority.rs` returns nothing. `SpiffeTraceChain` docs promise "Revocation propagation" (`escalation.rs:177`) with no field or code. X.509: "No revocation checking for now" (`crates/nucleus-identity/src/verifier.rs:319`, `tls.rs:319`). The only revocation-shaped control is `lockdown` (`crates/nucleus-node/src/main.rs:3688-3745`): a gRPC broadcast that flips an `AtomicBool` per tool-proxy (`crates/nucleus-tool-proxy/src/lockdown_client.rs:1-15`), scoped all/pod/label, effective only while the stream is up. `SignedTaskRef`, `ApprovalBundle`, escalation grants: expiry only. `TimeLattice::extend` widens `valid_until` with no monotonicity check (`crates/portcullis/src/time.rs:130-132`). Docs mention revocation only for OIDC keys (`docs/oidc-provider-runbook.md:207-215`).

**What SOTA does.** Biscuit derives a `revocation_id` per block (the block signature bytes) and verifiers keep a deny-list; UCAN has a separate `ucan/rvk` object any chain issuer may emit for downstream; OCapN caretakers revoke by killing the forwarder; PORTICO binds grants to epochs; Warner AI AGENT Act draft and IMDA both require "revocable".

**Direction.** (1) Define `revocation_id = block.signature` for every `AuthorityBlock`/`DelegationBlock`; add a `RevocationSet` to `verify_certificate`'s inputs (fail if any block id is listed) and to `VerifiedPermissions`. (2) Reuse the lockdown stream (already mTLS, already fan-out) to push signed `Revoke{ids, issuer, reason}` messages that the tool-proxy applies to its kernel: revoke *this* chain, not the fleet. Any principal in the chain may revoke downstream (UCAN rule); the node persists the set beside `pods/<id>/authority.json`. (3) Add an `epoch` caveat: the node advertises a signed epoch; a block carries `min_epoch`; bumping the epoch voids everything older (kill-switch with no list). (4) Remove `TimeLattice::extend` or make it `try_extend(ceiling)` refusing beyond the parent block. (5) Prove in Lean that revocation is monotone (a revoked prefix implies every descendant fails).

### G3 — The authorization language cannot say what principals mean: scope attenuation is spell-for-spell, parameters are one string, and there is no caveat language  [high / large / architectural]

**What is.**
- `SinkScope{allowed_paths, allowed_hosts, allowed_git_refs}` (`certificate.rs:454-467`) and `SinkScope::contains` uses exact string membership: `child.iter().all(|c| parent.iter().any(|p| p == c))` (`certificate.rs:492-504`). A child cannot narrow `/workspace/` to `/workspace/out/` — that fails verification. Meanwhile `DelegationScope::is_subset_of` in `portcullis-core` uses semantic `glob_covers` (`crates/portcullis-core/src/delegation.rs:215-232`), and `TokenScope` uses glob coverage too: three carriers, two containment semantics.
- The only per-request parameter reaching `Kernel::decide` is `subject: &str` (`kernel.rs:1125-1128`); `decide_term` lowers the `ActionTerm` to `(operation, subject)` (`kernel.rs:1516-1518`). Nothing can express "push only to `feature/*`", "≤ 3 `web_fetch` per host", "no `run_bash` after reading `.env`", "only counterparty X", "only 09:00–17:00", "only if receipt R present".
- The principal-facing schema (`ProfileSpec`, `crates/portcullis/src/profile.rs:50-96`) is capabilities × 3 levels + obligations + `paths{allowed,blocked}` + budget + time. No hosts, commands, counterparties, per-op counts, or conditions; the CEL `constraint` module is a separate, unwired `PolicySpec` (profile.rs:46-47 says so). `nucleus token delegate` takes only `profile`, `identity`, `expires_hours` — no scope, no budget split (`crates/nucleus-cli/src/token.rs:80-115`). `Intent` in the Rust SDK is an 11-variant enum (`crates/nucleus-sdk/src/intent.rs:19-31`).
- Extension tools are 3-level string keys outside the verified core (72-site Kani divergence per the formal map).

**What SOTA does.** Biscuit blocks carry Datalog `check if / check all / reject if` predicates with `trusting` scoping; UCAN `pol` constrains invocation args with selectors; RFC 9396 `authorization_details` types carry `actions/locations/datatypes`; Cedar gives a Lean-verified predicate language; Progent uses arg-constraining JSON-schema; gatehouse's `subsetGlob` is *semantic* containment via a kernel primitive (`/home/user/gatehouse/prelude/ci.writ:44-45`, `crates/writ-kernel/src/lib.rs:716 prim_glob_subsumes`) — the Rust `Cap::leq` there is still syntactic (`crates/gatehouse-types/src/lib.rs:84-96`), which is the same defect.

**Direction.** (1) Unify scope containment on semantic glob subsumption (borrow gatehouse's `globSubsumes` and its Lean proof; fix `SinkScope::contains` to use it; make `SinkScope` and `DelegationScope` and `TokenScope` one type). (2) Add a caveat block type to `DelegationBlock`: a small, total, decidable predicate language over the `ActionTerm` (op, path glob, host glob, git ref glob, argv prefix, counterparty id, per-op count/rate, time-of-day, "requires approval block with kid K"), with a defined meet (conjunction) so monotonicity is preserved and provable. (3) Extend `ProfileSpec` and `token delegate` to author caveats; extend `decide_term` to evaluate them on the full `PrimitiveAction`. (4) Prove caveat evaluation in Lean at Cedar's bar (small model + DRT against Rust).

### G4 — Delegation is a single-signer line: no third-party/co-signed blocks, no quorum, no seal, no identity continuity  [high / medium / architectural+implementation]

**What is.** Every `DelegationBlock` is signed by `prev_next_key` — the previous holder's ephemeral key — and nothing else (`certificate.rs:1383-1390`). A second principal, an approval service, or a budget service cannot contribute a block without holding the chain. `from_identity`/`to_identity` are signed but never checked for continuity (`block[i].from == block[i-1].to`) nor bound to an SVID inside the verifier (`certificate.rs:145-172, 1344-1440`; identity is bound only at the leaf by the node/proxy). `AttenuationToken::seal` is a serialization wrapper, not Biscuit's "no further attenuation" flag (`crates/portcullis/src/token.rs:160-200`). Multi-approver at runtime is explicitly "a roster, not a quorum: any single configured approver may approve" (`crates/nucleus-tool-proxy/src/auth.rs:486-488`). Thresholds exist only in the CI-only constitutional path (`crates/ck-kernel/src/lib.rs:445`, run in `Preflight` mode that skips signatures) and in `SignedDeclassify` for declassification. `SpiffeTraceLink.attestation` is a non-empty check (`escalation.rs:153-154, 359-362`), so `require_attestation` (`escalation.rs:719`) is satisfied by any bytes.

**What SOTA does.** Biscuit v3+ third-party blocks (signature covers `data_n + alg_n + pk_n`, `trusting` scopes facts to named keys) and `seal`; Macaroon third-party discharges separate "what" from "who"; UCAN `aud`/`iss` alignment per hop; AP2 requires the human's device key on cart mandates.

**Direction.** (1) Add `CosignedBlock{payload, signers: Vec<(kid, sig)>, trusting: Vec<kid>}` and a `min_signers` caveat so an approval or budget authority co-signs a hop without the root key. (2) Implement m-of-n in `verify_ed25519_any` → `verify_ed25519_threshold(keys, k, …)` and expose `approvers.threshold` in proxy config. (3) Check identity continuity in `verify_certificate` and bind `to_identity` to the SVID at *every* hop that is a pod (node already has the SVID). (4) Add a real `sealed: bool` to the last block. (5) Verify `attestation` bytes as a signature over `canonical_attestation_message` or delete the field.

### G5 — Budget and spend are not a credential dimension a principal can rely on: no conservation in the chain, no true-up, no per-op/per-counterparty/sequence limits, and none of it is proved  [high / medium / implementation]

**What is.** `PermissionLattice::delegate_to(&self, …)` checks `requested.budget.max_cost_usd > self.budget.remaining()` and decrements nothing (`crates/portcullis/src/lattice.rs:516-547`). `budget_ledger.rs:1-15` states it: "A parent that spawns N children … hands out up to N× that budget. No stateless credential can conserve a counter". Conservation lives only in the node's `BudgetLedger`, and "Until a child can report what it actually spent, release folds the WHOLE allocation into the parent's consumption" (`pod_authority.rs:46-56`). `BudgetSpec` is `max_cost_usd`, `max_input_tokens`, `max_output_tokens` (`profile.rs:85-94`): no per-op counts, no per-host/per-counterparty ceilings, no monetary ceiling on real-world sinks, no sliding-window limits. `TimeSpec` is a duration. Formal: "Time and budget checks — runtime checks with no formal model" (FORMAL_METHODS.md:149); the ledger Kani harnesses are nightly-only and the nightly OOMs (KANI-STATUS.md per formal map).

**What SOTA does.** Stripe Shared Payment Tokens: one seller, one currency, one max amount, one expiry, set at issuance and never growing; Mastercard Agentic Tokens bind agent+merchant+consent policy; ScopeGate's five-stage PDP includes a money ceiling and idempotency; "Before the Tool Call" (arXiv 2603.20953) adds sliding-window aggregates against structuring; IETF attenuating-agent-tokens pushes quotas to deployment shared state (which nucleus did) but pairs it with an allocation id in the token.

**Direction.** (1) Put a ledger `allocation_id` (node-signed) in the `DelegationBlock` so the credential *names* its conserved allocation and a verifier can check it against the ledger; add child-spend true-up on release (the tool-proxy already meters `charge`, `kernel.rs:1719`). (2) Extend `BudgetLattice` with per-op count and per-window (sliding) limits and a per-sink monetary ceiling; make meet = pointwise min. (3) Extract `LedgerCore` and the budget/time comparisons to Lean via the existing Aeneas pipeline (they are integer, heap-free — exactly the shape that extracts).

### G6 — Who authorized what is not a first-class, third-party-verifiable record: authorizations are not lineage edges, receipts don't bind the chain, and five carriers are unlinked  [high / medium / architectural]

**What is.** `EdgeKind` has PodAdmit, ToolCall, LlmCall, ArtifactProduced, Merge, DocumentRetrieved, economic kinds, Other — no Delegate/Approve/Escalate/Revoke (`crates/nucleus-lineage/src/edge.rs:287-412`; `grep -i approv|delegat` finds only a doc comment). Runtime approvals are logged with `ActorIdentity::Unknown` into a `VerdictSink` (tracing/OTLP), not a signed edge (`main.rs:4344-4351`). `EscalationGrant` is unsigned (`escalation.rs:513-530`). No receipt in `nucleus-receipt` or tool-proxy carries the certificate fingerprint (`grep fingerprint|cert` over `nucleus-receipt/src/lib.rs`, `nucleus-tool-proxy/src/receipt*.rs`: nothing; the only fingerprint use is `identity_fusion.rs:57-73`, an in-process check). The carriers — `LatticeCertificate`, `SignedTaskRef`, `SpiffeTraceChain`, `ApprovalBundle`, receipt `Session.parent_chain`, OIDC `act` — are not cross-referenced. So a third party holding receipts cannot answer "under which delegation, approved by whom, with what scope at each hop was this effect performed"; the best they get is a signed effect with an issuer kid.

**What SOTA does.** AP2 mandates are VCs chained intent → cart → payment with the human's device key; SCITT/in-toto DSSE receipts bind statement → log inclusion → cosigned checkpoint; EU AI Act Art. 12/14 (in force 2 Aug 2026) requires lifecycle logging of decision points and operator interactions; gatehouse receipts bind `gate_def, scope, env, tree, plan, verdict, signer_chain` and are refutable.

**Direction.** (1) Add `EdgeKind::{Delegate{cert_fp, hop}, Approve{approval_jti, approver_kid, args_hash}, Escalate{grant_id}, Revoke{ids}}`, emitted by the node (mint/re-root/ledger allocate/release) and by the proxy (approve/escalate/lockdown), signed with the existing edge signer and hash-chained. (2) Put `cert_fingerprint`, `approval_jti` and `allocation_id` into the receipt `capability` projection so `nucleus-verifier-service` and the wasm/py verifiers can check effect ⊆ chain offline. (3) Name the approver: derive `ActorIdentity` from the key that verified in `verify_ed25519_any`. (4) Publish the record shape as the Art. 12 export.

### G7 — Authority enters only as nucleus's own certificate rooted in the node key: no ingress for the authorization objects principals actually hold, and "human principal" is a string  [medium / large / architectural]

**What is.** `PodAuthority::admit` accepts exactly three callers: a registered pod, an external mTLS SPIFFE identity presenting `x-nucleus-delegation-cert` verified against a static `NUCLEUS_CERT_TRUST_ANCHORS` list, or the single bootstrap identity (`pod_authority.rs:24-42`). Request-borne certs are honoured only on the `SpiffeMtls` tier (`pod_cert.rs`). `grep -i authorization_details|IntentMandate|txn_token|"act"` over tool-proxy, node, portcullis and the SDK returns nothing; `cart.rs` deliberately "does not reimplement mandates" and verifies no signatures. `ApprovalBundle.iss` is a free-form SPIFFE string (`spiffe://…/human/alice`) with no binding to an IdP assertion, device key or WebAuthn credential. `docs/wimse-aims-conformance-gap.md` lists `typ/client_id/scope/act` as open. Capability trust anchors are unrelated to the SPIFFE federation bundles and the trust registry that nucleus already implements.

**What SOTA does.** MCP 2026-07 mandates RFC 8707 audience binding and RFC 8693 exchange per hop; Enterprise-Managed Authorization (ID-JAG) asserts user+groups+resource; RFC 9396 RAR carries typed `authorization_details`; Transaction Tokens for Agents carry `sub/act/purp/azd`; AP2 Intent Mandates are principal-signed VCs; GNAP has key-bound structured access rights. The protocols report's conclusion: the winning runtime is where any of these is verified and *intersected* into one monotone grant.

**Direction.** (1) A `Grant` ingress in `nucleus-node`: adapters that verify an OAuth/RAR token, ID-JAG assertion, txn-token with `act`, AP2 Intent Mandate, or UCAN/Biscuit chain, map it to a `PermissionLattice + SinkScope + caveats + principal`, and mint an `AuthorityBlock` whose `provenance` is the hash of the source object (re-root already exists for certs). (2) Bind the human principal: `ApprovalBundle.iss`/approval blocks must be signed by a key attested by an IdP token exchange or a WebAuthn/device key registered via the trust registry. (3) Derive certificate trust anchors from SPIFFE federation bundles rather than a static list.

### G8 — The clause-3 primitives are the least-proved part of the system, and gatehouse's ceiling has no principal or monotone gate at all  [medium / medium / measurement+implementation]

**What is.** Nucleus: the wire verifier (`verify_certificate` signature/hash/expiry/scope walk) is outside Lean; the only extracted theorem is the monotone `leq` walk with `Iter::fold` as an axiom; the three certificate Kani harnesses stub crypto (`crates/portcullis/src/kani/certificate_harnesses.rs:167-272`) and run nightly where the job OOMs. Approvals, escalation, revocation (n/a), time and budget are HAND or TEST (FORMAL_METHODS.md:149; formal map). `WithinDelegationCeiling` is "dormant … requested == ceiling, so its check does not yet constrain anything" (`reviews/north-star-certificate.md:146-148`). Gatehouse: the ceiling is a `let` in the PR's own tree (`.gatehouse/pipeline.writ:9`) and `PUT /v1/{tenant}/plan` (`crates/gatehouse-controld/src/lib.rs:956-981`) is guarded by one shared bearer with no monotonicity check and no signer identity, while nucleus's `governance_monotone` (`crates/nucleus-policy-kernel`) is exactly that check with no consumer. The Rust `Cap::leq` is syntactic (`gatehouse-types/src/lib.rs:84-96`) while the kernel-checked writ order is semantic.

**What SOTA does.** Cedar: small Lean model + differential random testing against Rust, proofs over the evaluator; gatehouse's own `check_sound` shows how to make the *decision* a proof object; Biscuit ships cross-implementation conformance vectors.

**Direction.** (1) Extract the structural part of `verify_certificate` (hash-chain, expiry, scope, revocation set, caveat meet) to Lean with signatures as opaque primitives, gatehouse-style (one axiom per primitive, golden differential). (2) Make `WithinDelegationCeiling` bite by passing the *requested* lattice from the `ActionTerm`. (3) In gatehouse: sign the ceiling with a principal key, run `governance_monotone`/`leqCap` on `put_plan` (widening requires a co-signed change), and make `Cap::leq` semantic. (4) Add a clause-3 ratchet: count of authorization primitives (caveat kinds, revocation, quorum, ingress adapters) covered by an extracted proof and a conformance vector.

## 2. Why these eight, in this order

Ordered by how much safely-delegatable agency each unlocks. G1 and G2 are the two things a principal cannot do today at all (approve a *specific* action and revoke what they granted); without them every widening is either blocked or unbounded. G3 is what lets a principal say "yes, but only…" in enough detail that the runtime can auto-approve most work (the 80/15/5 pattern) rather than escalate. G4–G6 make delegation composable (co-signers, quorum), conserved (budget) and accountable (edges, receipts), which is what lets authority cross organizations and survive audit. G7 is interop — necessary for adoption but built on G1–G6. G8 is the proof and ratchet layer that turns each of the above into a "structurally incapable" claim rather than a feature.

Deliberately not listed as separate gaps (already covered by other clause-owners or subsumed): the vendor-specific runner, the `run` wire mismatch, Tier-1 secret-on-disk self-approval (a G1 consequence), the `SpiffeTraceChain` attestation bytes (folded into G4), stale docs.
